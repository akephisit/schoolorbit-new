# Curriculum publication history

Approved scope: the user's eight-point publication/amendment plan, accepted with “โอเค”. The completed edition → level → program hierarchy stays canonical. Revision year identifies the school curriculum, whereas publication number identifies a released content set. No actual second-term academic content is invented.

## Ownership and lifecycle

Academic Core owns the edition, one amendment workspace, validation, atomic publication and immutable whole-edition history. Keep this capability in the existing school-academic-core crate: publication shares its transaction, validation, permissions and models; a separate crate would expose the same implementation internals without improving invalidation scope. Delivery owns its actual offering graphs and provenance; Timetable continues consuming immutable Delivery graphs.

Existing edition, educational-level and program UUIDs remain stable. Existing relational curriculum tables become the single mutable workspace, writable only while the edition has a draft token. Immutable normalized publication tables own released content. Published consumers read views selecting the edition's current publication, never the workspace. Explicit draft-token reads expose the workspace to editors. Initial never-published editions have a draft from creation. Opening an amendment reuses the edition's sole draft and invalidates stale workspace row versions. Every content mutation requires that exact draft token. Publishing locks the edition, validates all levels and plans, captures the complete graph and closes the draft atomically; failed validation consumes no publication number.

Publication headers store the number, previous publication, revision metadata, change note, publisher and publication date. Baseline migration records known dates and leaves unknown actors/dates unknown. Snapshot rows preserve all original content and IDs. Database guards forbid historical updates/deletes or inserts after the capture transaction. History supports read-only structure and a semantic comparison against its previous publication.

Requirement source identities outlive a workspace row so replacing draft requirements cannot delete an actual offering's historical source. Published snapshot requirements and live offering details reference these durable identities. Legacy actual delivery/timetable graphs are left byte-for-byte unchanged, with provenance explicitly unknown where no historical evidence exists. New curriculum preparation records the publications actually read, includes them in its preview hash and persists them when applied. Delivery graphs carry the recorded provenance through publication/cloning. Rooms choose edition/program and do not choose publication numbers.

## Authorization and UI

Use existing school-wide curriculum read/manage permissions; management is enforced on every amendment and publication route. No organization-unit ownership or new permission family. Normal routes show published content; “แก้ไขหลักสูตร” opens the draft. The edition remains labeled by Buddhist revision year, with “เผยแพร่ครั้งที่ …”, a separate draft badge and “ประวัติการแก้ไข”. History is read-only. Draft editors retain term, plan, course/activity and import tools, with cancellation and stale-response protection. Add/remove requirements and term 2 only through explicit saved edits.
